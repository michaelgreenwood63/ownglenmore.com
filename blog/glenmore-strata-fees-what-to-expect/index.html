Strata Fees Across Glenmore's Condo & Townhome Buildings: What to Expect

November 2026 · By Mark Jontz & Associates · 5 min read

Glenmore has 15 named condo and townhome buildings we track, ranging from a 30-unit 55+ building built in 1982 to a 277-unit pre-construction project expected in 2026. Strata fees across that range vary a lot, and the listing price alone won't tell you what you're actually signing up for every month. Here's what actually drives the number, and what to do before you rely on it.

Building Age Cuts Both Ways

An older building like Glenmore Place (built 1982) or GlenMeadows (built 1991) has had decades to build up — or fail to build up — a healthy reserve fund, and older mechanical systems (roofing, elevators, plumbing stacks) eventually need replacing regardless of how well a building has been maintained. That can mean higher ongoing fees to fund a properly reserved building, or it can mean a special assessment if a previous council underfunded the reserve for years. Age alone doesn't tell you which situation you're looking at — the reserve fund study does.

A newer building like Trellis (built 2022) or the pre-construction Eminence at Knox Mountain (expected 2026) typically starts with lower fees simply because nothing has needed major replacement yet. That's not a permanent advantage — it just means the bigger capital expenses are still ahead rather than behind.

Amenities and Shared Infrastructure

More shared infrastructure generally means more cost to maintain it. A larger, multi-building complex like The Verve (8 buildings, 441 units) has more shared systems and common areas to budget for than a smaller single building, though it also splits those costs across far more units. A gated community like SandPointe carries the cost of maintaining that gate and the associated grounds. None of this is a red flag by itself — it's just part of what a higher or lower fee is actually paying for, and worth understanding rather than just comparing the raw number.

Number of Units to Split the Cost Across

A mid-size building like Glenmore Central (105 units) or GlenMeadows (100 units) spreads a given maintenance budget across more owners than a smaller building like The Peaks (35 units) or Glenmore Place (30 units). All else being equal, more units sharing the same fixed costs (elevator service contracts, landscaping, insurance) usually means a lower fee per unit — but "all else being equal" is doing a lot of work in that sentence, since reserve fund health and amenity level vary independently of unit count.

None of the building facts above tell you an actual dollar figure. We don't publish specific fee numbers here because they change, and because the only number that actually matters is the current one for the specific unit you're looking at — not a general estimate for the building. What we can tell you from the factors above is roughly what's driving whichever number you're looking at, so you can ask the right follow-up questions.

Always Pull the Form B

Before you remove subjects on any strata purchase in BC, you're entitled to a Form B — Information Certificate — from the strata corporation. It discloses the current monthly fee, the reserve fund balance, any pending or approved special assessments, bylaw amendments, and whether the strata is involved in any litigation. The listing sheet gives you a snapshot; the Form B gives you the real financial picture as of the request date. Always get it, and read the whole thing, not just the fee line.
